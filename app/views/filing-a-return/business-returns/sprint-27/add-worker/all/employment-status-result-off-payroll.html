This is based on the information provided.

Why you are getting this result

The answers provided indicate that the worker:

  • will carry out duties as an office holder
  • provides their services through an intermediary, such as a limited company, partnership or unincorporated body

The worker is treated as employed for tax purposes for this work.

For this example, the intermediary needs to operate PAYE on the worker’s earnings.

What you should do next

  1. Save or print this result.
  2. Share it with the person responsible for handling the worker’s payments.
  3. Keep a copy with the answers provided for your records.
{{ govukInsetText({ text: "Do not add payments for this work to your return as self-employed worker payments. They need to be handled through PAYE." }) }}

Adding this person as an employee

If you are taking this person on as an employee of your business, add them to your payroll rather than this return.

  1. Get their personal details and P45. If they do not have a P45, use HMRC’s starter checklist to work out their tax code and starter declaration.
  2. Set them up in your payroll software.
  3. Tell HMRC about them by including their details in your first Full Payment Submission (FPS) for them, on or before their first payday.

Include their personal details, tax code, starter declaration, and pay and deductions since they started working for you. Do not include pay or deductions from their previous job.

Find out how to register a new employee with HMRC.

If the person continues to work through an intermediary, share this result with the person responsible for payroll so they can arrange the appropriate PAYE treatment.

If the working arrangement changes, check the worker’s employment status again.
